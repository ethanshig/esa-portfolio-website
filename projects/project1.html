Sustainable Housing Complex

Type Residential
Year 2024
Location Concept Design
Role Lead Designer

Project Overview

This net-zero energy residential development reimagines multi-family housing through the lens of sustainability and community. The design integrates passive solar principles, local materials, and regenerative site planning to create homes that give back to the environment.

The complex features 24 units arranged around shared green spaces, promoting social interaction while maximizing natural light and ventilation. Rooftop solar panels and ground-source heat pumps eliminate dependence on fossil fuels.

Challenge

How can we create affordable, beautiful housing that operates at net-zero energy while fostering community connection in an urban context?

Solution

The design employs a courtyard typology that maximizes shared outdoor space while maintaining privacy. South-facing units optimize solar gain, while deep overhangs and deciduous trees provide summer shading. Natural ventilation strategies reduce cooling loads by 60% compared to conventional apartments.

Sustainability Features

  • Net-zero energy performance through passive design and renewable energy
  • Cross-laminated timber (CLT) construction reducing embodied carbon by 40%
  • Rainwater harvesting system providing 80% of irrigation needs
  • Native plant landscaping supporting local pollinators and wildlife
  • Community composting and urban agriculture areas
  • Electric vehicle charging stations and bike storage

Outcomes & Impact

This design demonstrates that sustainable housing can be both beautiful and economically viable. Energy modeling shows residents would save an average of $2,400 annually on utilities while reducing carbon emissions by 15 tons per household per year.

The project received recognition in the 2024 Sustainable Design Awards and has been featured in architectural publications as a model for climate-conscious urban housing.
